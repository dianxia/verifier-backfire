\begin{abstract}
Test-time verification is becoming a standard component of LLM
agent systems: a generator produces an artifact, a verifier
accepts or rejects it, and rejections trigger repair.  We give a
cost--correctness theory of this generation--verification--repair
(GVR) loop---including a correction of a tempting over-reading of
it---and stress both in a two-benchmark study with full
silent-oracle instrumentation: one generator (qwen-plus), six LLM
judge configurations spanning three model families, unit-test
oracles, budget $N{=}5$, $164 \times 9$ HumanEval and $247 \times 9$
MBPP runs, every attempt ground-truth-scored.  On the theory side, misses hurt
final precision $R = a/(1-b)$ times as much per unit as false
rejections when retries redraw at the generator's rate; degraded
retries ($q < q^\ast$) flip that ranking; and---the
correction---per-event dominance of false rejections does not by
itself imply backfire: the loop backfires iff its delivered-correct
probability $A_N$ falls below the deliver-first-attempt baseline,
equivalent in the steady state to $R_q < (1-a)/b$.  Empirically the
backfire is real, severe, and sharply patterned by judge
configuration.  Same-family judges lose correct work---net $-15$
correct solutions for the isolated self-judge on HumanEval
($p=.0007$), $-10$ for the cross-capability judge on both
benchmarks---while neither cross-family configuration
(deepseek-v4-pro, glm-5.2) shows a reliable negative effect, and
glm-5.2 trends positive on MBPP ($+8$, nominal $p=.039$).  A
same-family reasoning-judge control dissociates the axes: the
collapse signature persists within the family, but the
significant losses are confined to the non-reasoning judges.
The mechanism is not broken repair: retries succeed at
$q=.47$--$.95$, but same-family judges' acceptance of correct work
collapses across rounds (round 1: $.75$--$.87$; round $\ge 4$:
$.00$--$.36$) although the judges cannot see the round---the
empirical signature of instance-persistent verifier--generator
correlation, one of two failure regimes our theory separates; the
other, priced by the flip condition, governs the cross-capability
arm, and the corrected condition, evaluated at the experiment's
budget, signs all twelve judge-benchmark cells.  Oracle verification
never loses a correct solution ($+20$/$+17$ rescues on MBPP,
$p<10^{-4}$; leak exactly zero wherever $b{=}1$), and fully paired
feedback and verifier-context ablations yield no statistically
reliable effect.  The engineering lesson is sharp: whether a loop
purifies or grinds is governed by the verifier's configuration
relative to the generator---family-associated in our arms---and
its acceptance persistence on resubmitted work---measure both
before trusting any verifier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Production LLM agent systems increasingly wrap generation in a
verification loop: an executor produces an artifact, a verifier
judges it, rejections trigger repair, and the loop terminates when
the verifier accepts or the budget runs out.  This
generation--verification--repair (GVR) pattern underlies executor/
verifier pipelines, LLM-as-judge filters, and test-driven agent
loops.  The engineering question it raises is sharp and often
ignored: \emph{when does adding a verifier actually improve the
final answer, and when does it make things worse?}

Two literatures border this question without answering it: the
self-correction literature documents that intrinsic self-correction
frequently fails while sound external verification helps
\citep{stechly2024selfverify,kamoi2024when,huang2024cannot}, and the
test-time-scaling theory of \citet{dorner2026rocnreroll} covers pure
resampling---rejection carries no feedback, retries are independent
redraws, and no operating-point guidance is given.  Neither
analyzes the repair loop with feedback.

This paper contributes a compact theory of the GVR loop and a
two-benchmark, nine-arm study ($164 \times 9$ HumanEval;
$247 \times 9$ MBPP) with one generator, six LLM judge
configurations from three model families, and silent-oracle
instrumentation of every attempt.  Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A corrected backfire theory
(§\ref{sec:asym}--\ref{sec:degrade}).}  The miss-asymmetry exchange
rate $R = a/(1-b)$; its degradation modulation $R_q$ and per-event
flip threshold $q^\ast$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:flip}); and the terminal
backfire condition (Corollary~\ref{cor:backfire}): the loop
backfires iff $A_N(p,a,b,q) < p$, equivalent in the steady state to
$R_q < (1-a)/b$.  Per-event dominance of false rejections does not
by itself imply backfire---a verifier whose catches rescue enough
wrong work stays above the baseline even with $q < q^\ast$.
\item \textbf{Configuration-dependent backfire,
family-associated in our arms (§\ref{sec:exp}).}  Under identical
tasks, prompts, generator budget, and instrumentation, same-family
judges lose correct work (isolated self-judge $-15$ on HumanEval,
$p=.0007$; cross-capability judge $-10$ on both benchmarks), while
neither cross-family configuration shows a significant negative
effect and glm-5.2 trends positive on MBPP ($+8$, nominal
$p=.039$/$.012$).  On HumanEval the cross-family judges are
near-oracles (leak $.012$ at $\hat a + \hat b \approx 1.7$).  The
cross-family judges are also stronger, reasoning models---a
co-varying confound a dedicated control arm dissects: the
same-family judge served with thinking enabled and the
cross-family verdict budget still collapses and clusters, yet
nets $-3$/$-4$ (n.s.), so the significant backfires require the
same-family correlation \emph{and} the non-reasoning operating
point.
\item \textbf{The mechanism: acceptance collapse, not broken
repair (§\ref{sec:exp}).}  Retries succeed at $q=.47$--$.95$, yet
same-family judges' acceptance of correct attempts collapses with
round ($\hat a_1 \approx .75$--$.87$,
$\hat a_{k \ge 4} \approx .00$--$.36$) although the isolated
judges cannot see the round, and their rejections of correct work
cluster by task---evidence consistent with the instance-persistent
correlation that Theorem~\ref{thm:decorr} prices.  The terminal
backfire condition, evaluated at the experiment's budget, signs
all twelve judge-benchmark cells; the pooled two-stage model flags
the self-judge arms only marginally and underpredicts their losses
by an order of magnitude---the gap is the collapse itself.
\item \textbf{Nulls and instrumentation (§\ref{sec:exp}).}
A fully paired feedback ablation (shared failing code, McNemar)
detects no feedback-content effect ($p = .50$/$.55$ on the two
benchmarks), leaving $\delta > 0$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:delta}) undetermined; a verifier-context
probe again finds no collapse; single-shot verification purifies
the delivered stream in all 12 judge-benchmark cells; and
completion-cap instrumentation matters---a 1{,}024-token pilot
truncated 36--68\% of round-2 completions by arm on HumanEval
into unparsable code, masquerading as repair degradation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Self-correction and its critiques.}
Self-Refine \citep{madaan2023selfrefine} and Reflexion
\citep{shinn2023reflexion} popularized iterative self-improvement;
\citet{stechly2024selfverify} documented verifier false-rejection
pathologies, with \citet{huang2024cannot} and the critical survey
of \citet{kamoi2024when} reaching compatible conclusions.  Our
theory gives these observations a probabilistic form: collapse
corresponds to failure-mode correlation between generator and
verifier (§\ref{sec:mixture}), and our experiment delimits where
that correlation appears.

\paragraph{Test-time scaling theory.}
\citet{dorner2026rocnreroll} prove that rejection sampling and
Best-of-$N$ accuracy is determined by verifier ROC geometry, and
that RS dominates BoN at fixed compute.  Their setting is pure
resampling: no feedback, no repair, no generator--verifier
correlation analysis, and no operating-point selection guidance.
Our paper is a deliberate three-axis extension: repair feedback
(§\ref{sec:repair}), operating-point calibration
(§\ref{sec:asym}), and degraded/correlated retries
(§\ref{sec:degrade},~\ref{sec:cluster}).  Related theory includes
inference-time alignment coverage analysis
\citep{huang2025bestofn}.  In the training-time regime,
\citet{plesner2026imperfect} study the robustness of RLVR training
to noisy verifiers and report that verifier precision matters more
than recall; our Theorem~\ref{thm:asym} supplies the inference-time
counterpart of that asymmetry in closed form.

\paragraph{Verification techniques.}
Self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency} uses agreement as
the verification signal; process supervision
\citep{lightman2024verify} trains step-level verifiers;
LLM-as-judge evaluation is surveyed by \citet{zheng2023judging}.
Control-theoretic formalizations of self-correction dynamics appear
in \citet{wu2026cybercorrect}; they manage loop stability, whereas
we manage the verifier's error budget.  All of these techniques
select a point on the verifier's quality trade-off; our results say
\emph{which} point to select and when the loop tolerates a harsh
verifier.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}

Table~\ref{tab:notation} (Appendix~\ref{app:notation}) summarizes
the paper's notation.

\subsection{Setup: the GVR loop}\label{sec:setup}

One \emph{attempt}: a generator produces an artifact; a verifier
either \emph{accepts} or \emph{rejects} it (with feedback); a
rejection triggers a new attempt, up to budget $N$.

\begin{definition}[Per-attempt rates]\label{def:rates}
Let correctness of an attempt be binary, with generator success
$p = \Pr(C)$.  The verifier has acceptance rate
$a = \Pr(\text{accept} \mid C)$ and catch rate
$b = \Pr(\text{reject} \mid \neg C)$.  Its two error types are the
\emph{miss} $1-b$ (accepting wrong work) and the \emph{false
rejection} $1-a$ (rejecting correct work).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Attempt outcomes]\label{def:outcomes}
Per attempt: $s = pa$ (accept-correct),
$\ell = (1-p)(1-b)$ (\emph{leak}: accept-wrong), and
$r = 1 - s - \ell$ (reject).
\end{definition}

Terminal states: \emph{accepted} (split into correct and leak) and
\emph{unresolved} (budget exhausted).  We assume (A1) the verdict is
independent of the generation path given correctness and (A2)
attempts are i.i.d.\ with constant $(p,a,b)$; both are relaxed in
§\ref{sec:repair}--\ref{sec:cluster}.

\begin{lemma}[Single-pass composition]\label{lem:single}
For $N=1$, $\Pr(\text{deliver wrong}) = (1-p)(1-b)$: a wrong
deliverable requires the conjunction of two independent failures.
The false-rejection rate does not enter this quantity.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct computation of $\Pr(\neg C \wedge \text{accept})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Loop closed forms]\label{thm:loop}
Under (A1)--(A2), with budget $N \ge 1$:
\begin{align}
\Pr(\text{leak}) &= \ell\,\frac{1-r^N}{1-r}, \quad
\Pr(\text{accepted}) = (s+\ell)\frac{1-r^N}{1-r},\\
\Pr(\text{unresolved}) &= r^N, \qquad
\mathbb{E}[\text{attempts}] = \frac{1-r^N}{1-r}.
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The loop is geometric: reaching attempt $k$ requires $k-1$
rejections (probability $r^{k-1}$); summing and telescoping gives
the claims.  The instance-level structure matches the ROC-based
characterization of \citet{dorner2026rocnreroll} for the pure
resampling case; we state it in outcome-decomposed form for use
below.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Budget--precision invariance]\label{thm:invariance}
For every $N\ge1$,
\[
\pi := \Pr(\text{wrong} \mid \text{accepted})
= \frac{(1-p)(1-b)}{pa + (1-p)(1-b)},
\]
independent of $N$: added budget converts unresolved mass into
accepted mass \emph{at fixed precision}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Ratio of the leak and accepted probabilities in
Theorem~\ref{thm:loop}; the geometric factors cancel.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The miss-asymmetry theorem}\label{sec:asym}

Both verifier errors hurt $\pi$---misses first-order through $\ell$,
false rejections second-order: each forced retry buys a fresh chance
to leak.  Their marginal damages have a closed-form ratio.

\begin{theorem}[Miss-asymmetry]\label{thm:asym}
\[
\frac{\partial \pi / \partial (1-b)}{\partial \pi / \partial (1-a)}
= \frac{a}{1-b}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\partial\pi/\partial(1-b) = (1-p)s/(s+\ell)^2$ and
$\partial\pi/\partial(1-a) = p\ell/(s+\ell)^2$ by the chain rule on
Definition~\ref{def:outcomes}; the ratio simplifies to $a/(1-b)$.
\end{proof}

At $(a,b)=(0.95,0.95)$, $R \approx 19$: one unit of catch is
worth $\sim$19 units of acceptance for precision, while $1-a$
enters expected attempts first-order and $1-b$ only through the
negligible $\ell$ term.

\begin{corollary}[Calibration guideline]\label{cor:calibrate}
Under (A2), any infinitesimal verifier modification---or one small
enough that the operating point moves little---trading acceptance
for catch at a local exchange rate better than $1{:}R$ improves
final precision to first order; its price is paid only in
attempts.  Calibrate verifiers
aggressively toward catch rate; budget false rejections rather than
fear them.  The qualifier is essential: when retries are degraded
below $q^\ast$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:flip}) the effective exchange rate
drops below 1 and the advice inverts---protect acceptance.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Majority voting]\label{cor:majority}
Replace a verifier by a majority vote of $k$ conditionally
independent verifiers (strict majority rejects; ties accept).  For
$a, b > 1/2$ the vote raises \emph{both} effective rates:
\[
b^{(k)} = \sum_{j>k/2}\binom{k}{j}b^j(1-b)^{k-j} > b, \qquad
a^{(k)} = 1 - \sum_{j>k/2}\binom{k}{j}(1-a)^j a^{k-j} > a,
\]
because majority rule makes false rejections \emph{rarer}, not
commoner---a lone objecting judge no longer suffices to reject.
Precision improves through both channels, and generator attempts
do not increase ($r$ falls); the price is $k$ verifier calls per
round (majority-of-three at the pooled self-judge point:
generator attempts $1.38 \to 1.25$, verifier calls $1.38 \to
3.75$, steady state under (A2), $<1\%$ change at $N{=}5$).
Unanimity-style rules (reject if any judge objects) trade the
other way: $a^{(k)} = a^k$ falls sharply while $b^{(k)} =
1-(1-b)^k$ rises.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Correlated failures}\label{sec:mixture}

(A1) fails in a specific direction: a verifier that is the same
model, primed with the generator's context, tends to repeat the
generator's mistake.  Model this with a latent failure-mode mixture.

\begin{definition}[Failure-mode mixture]\label{def:mixture}
Conditioned on latent mode $M=m$ with prior $\pi_m$, generation
succeeds with $p_m$; the verifier accepts correct output with
$a_m$ and catches wrong output with $b_m$, independently within
mode.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Effective rates]\label{def:beff}
With $\epsilon = \sum_m \pi_m(1-p_m)$, error mass
$\mu_m = \pi_m(1-p_m)/\epsilon$ and success mass
$\sigma_m = \pi_m p_m/(1-\epsilon)$:
$b_{\mathrm{eff}} = \sum_m \mu_m b_m$ is the error-mass-weighted
catch rate and $a_{\mathrm{eff}} = \sum_m \sigma_m a_m$ the
success-mass-weighted acceptance rate.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Decorrelation condition]\label{thm:decorr}
Attempt-1 outcomes obey the closed forms of §\ref{sec:setup} with
pooled rates $(p,a,b) \to (1-\epsilon,\, a_{\mathrm{eff}},\,
b_{\mathrm{eff}})$; in particular the accepted-stream error
\[
\pi_1 = \frac{\epsilon\,(1-b_{\mathrm{eff}})}
{\epsilon\,(1-b_{\mathrm{eff}}) + (1-\epsilon)\,a_{\mathrm{eff}}}
\]
improves on no verification (delivered error $\epsilon$) iff
\[
a_{\mathrm{eff}} + b_{\mathrm{eff}} > 1,
\]
i.e., iff the mass-weighted operating point lies above the chance
diagonal of ROC space: verification helps exactly when
$\Pr(\text{accept}\mid C) > \Pr(\text{accept}\mid \neg C)$.  Catch
alone does not suffice: if $b_{\mathrm{eff}} \to 0$ while
$a_{\mathrm{eff}} < 1$, verification is \emph{strictly harmful}
($\pi_1 = \epsilon/[\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)a_{\mathrm{eff}}] >
\epsilon$), degenerating to neutral theater only in the
rubber-stamp limit $a_{\mathrm{eff}} \to 1$.  Correlated failure has
an exact form: with $\nu = \sum_m \pi_m(1-b_m)$ the prior-weighted
miss rate,
\[
\Pr(\text{wrong} \wedge \text{miss})
= \epsilon(1-b_{\mathrm{eff}})
= \epsilon\nu + \mathrm{Cov}_m(1-p_m,\,1-b_m),
\]
the covariance over modes under prior $\pi_m$: decorrelation is
precisely the claim that this covariance be small.
\end{theorem}

For $N>1$ the mixture bites: with instance-persistent modes,
surviving to round $k$ conditions on the mode mix.  §\ref{sec:exp}
validates the attempt-1 form directly and then observes the
mixture in the raw: judges that cannot see the round nonetheless
accept correct work at collapsing per-round rates as rounds
accumulate---the signature of instance-persistent low-$a_m$ modes
conditioning the surviving pool.

\subsection{Repair feedback breaks i.i.d.\ --- favorably}\label{sec:repair}

\begin{proposition}[Repair factor]\label{prop:delta}
Let rejected attempts be followed by a repaired attempt succeeding
with $p' = p + \delta(1-p)$, $\delta \in [0,1]$ ($\delta=0$:
rejection sampling; $\delta=1$: perfect repair).  Then all
formulas of §\ref{sec:asym} with constant $p$ are conservative
(actual leak is upper-bounded), and the invariance of
Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance} breaks in the favorable direction:
precision improves with budget.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Sample a uniform $u$ once per attempt slot and couple a repaired
draw (correct iff $u \le p'$) with a fresh one (correct iff
$u \le p$); since $p' \ge p$, the repaired draw is correct whenever
the fresh one is.  Every round $k \ge 2$ therefore has masses
$s' = p'a \ge s$ and $\ell' = (1-p')(1-b) \le \ell$, so its
accepted stream is at least as pure,
$\pi' = \ell'/(s'+\ell') \le \pi$, and every constant-$p$ formula
is an upper bound on realized leak.  The terminal conditional
error at budget $N$ is a weighted mean of $\pi$ (round 1) and
$\pi'$ (rounds $\ge 2$) with the $\pi'$-weight strictly increasing
in $N$; it therefore decreases toward $\pi'$---the favorable break
of Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance}'s invariance.
\end{proof}

\noindent The bound isolates the repair channel: the verifier's
$(a,b)$ are held fixed, and repair is assumed to act on generator
success alone---it does not speak to verifier--generator
correlation (§\ref{sec:mixture}).

\subsection{Repair degradation flips the asymmetry}\label{sec:degrade}

Real repairs do not redraw at the generator's first-attempt rate:
the retry pool is conditioned on rejection, enriched for hard
instances, and its success rate $q$ can fall far below $p$---mildly
for some judges in our study, severely for oracle-side repair on
the harder benchmark.

\begin{definition}[Retry rate]\label{def:q}
$q := \Pr(\text{attempt } k{+}1 \text{ correct} \mid \text{attempt }
k \text{ rejected})$, $k \ge 1$.
\end{definition}

A falsely rejected \emph{correct} solution enters a sub-loop with
per-round success $q$; conditional on resolution it ends wrong with
\[
\pi_q = \frac{(1-q)(1-b)}{qa + (1-q)(1-b)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Modulated exchange rate]\label{thm:flip}
At the population level, the marginal damage ratio becomes
\[
R_q \;=\; \frac{a}{1-b}\cdot\frac{q(1-p)}{p(1-q)}.
\]
The asymmetry \emph{flips} (false rejections dominate) iff
\[
q < q^\ast \;=\; \frac{(1-b)p}{(1-p)a + (1-b)p}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A unit $\varepsilon$ of extra miss rate moves $\varepsilon(1-p)$
mass of wrong first attempts from ``caught'' (sub-loop wrong with
probability $\pi_q$) to ``delivered wrong'' (probability 1):
damage $\varepsilon(1-p)(1-\pi_q)$.  A unit of extra false-rejection
rate moves $\varepsilon p$ mass of correct first attempts from
``delivered correct'' to the sub-loop: damage $\varepsilon p \pi_q$.
Substituting $\pi_q = \ell_q/(s_q+\ell_q)$ with $s_q = qa$,
$\ell_q = (1-q)(1-b)$ and taking the ratio yields $R_q$; setting
$R_q = 1$ and solving for $q$ gives $q^\ast$.  For $q=p$ the
expression recovers Theorem~\ref{thm:asym}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Terminal backfire condition]\label{cor:backfire}
In the two-stage model (round 1 at $p$, retries at $q$, constant
$(a,b)$), the delivered-correct probability at budget $N$ is
\[
A_N \;=\; pa \;+\; r_1\, qa\, \frac{1-r_q^{N-1}}{1-r_q},
\qquad
r_1 = p(1-a) + (1-p)b, \qquad
r_q = q(1-a) + (1-q)b,
\]
and the loop backfires relative to the deliver-first-attempt baseline
iff $A_N < p$.  In the infinite-budget limit this is equivalent to
\[
R_q \;<\; \frac{1-a}{b}:
\]
terminal backfire requires the modulated exchange rate to fall below
$(1-a)/b$---for verifiers above the chance line ($a+b>1$, hence
$(1-a)/b<1$), a strictly deeper drop than the per-event flip at
$R_q=1$.  Per-event dominance of false rejections does not by itself
imply backfire: a verifier whose catches convert enough wrong first
attempts into rescued correct work can remain above the baseline even
with $q < q^\ast$.  The conversion rate of correct solutions into
wrong ones remains $(1-a)\,p\,\pi_q$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Round 1 delivers correct mass $pa$; reaching sub-round $k \ge 2$
requires the round-1 rejection mass $r_1$ followed by $k-2$ sub-loop
rejections ($r_q$ each) and delivers $qa$; summing the geometric
series gives $A_N$.  For the limit, $A_\infty < p$ iff
\[
r_1\,qa < p(1-a)\bigl[qa+(1-q)(1-b)\bigr]
\iff
(1-p)\,b\,q\,a < p\,(1-a)\,(1-q)\,(1-b),
\]
and substituting $R_q$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:flip} turns the
right-hand condition into $R_q < (1-a)/b$.  Since $A_N < A_\infty$ for
every finite $N$, the steady-state condition is sufficient for
backfire at any budget (finite $N$ only enlarges the backfire
region).
\end{proof}

\subsection{What pooled rates cannot see}\label{sec:cluster}

The pooled $(\hat a, \hat b)$ reported by evaluation studies
summarize attempts across instances and rounds.  Three phenomena
hide beneath the summary: (i) retry degradation
(§\ref{sec:degrade}) multiplies per-retry leak chance from
$(1-p)(1-b)$ to $(1-q)(1-b)$; (ii) misses cluster on particular
wrong-solution instances that recur across rounds, so
instance-level modes with low $b_m$ carry disproportionate mass;
and (iii) acceptance can collapse on particular \emph{correct}
instances---modes with low $a_m$ that survive every round---which
the pooled $\hat a$ averages away.  §\ref{sec:exp} measures (iii)
directly, uses (i) to bound (ii), and shows that the magnitude of
pooled-rate failure is itself instrumentation-sensitive: pooled
theory understates terminal leak only mildly in our clean runs,
and a completion-cap artifact can inflate the apparent failure
substantially.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Two benchmarks: HumanEval \citep{chen2021humaneval}, all 164
problems, and MBPP \citep{austin2021mbpp}, 247 tasks from the
hand-verified subset of the official test split (task IDs
11--510).  Generator: qwen-plus (temperature 1.0), prompted to
plan then emit a Python block; budget $N{=}5$.  Nine arms:
\textsf{none} (deliver first attempt); \textsf{oracle\_full} /
\textsf{oracle\_verdict} (unit tests in a timeout-limited
subprocess; feedback = failing-test traceback / verdict only); and
six LLM judge configurations---\textsf{judge\_hetero} (qwen-max),
\textsf{judge\_self\_iso} and \textsf{judge\_self\_ctx} (qwen-plus,
the generator's own family; the latter sees the generator's
reasoning), two cross-family judges,
\textsf{judge\_deepseek\_iso} (deepseek-v4-pro) and
\textsf{judge\_glm\_iso} (glm-5.2), and a same-family
reasoning-judge control, \textsf{judge\_self\_iso\_thinking}
(qwen-plus with thinking enabled, 4{,}096-token verdict budget),
added to dissect the family/capability confound.  Every attempt is
additionally
scored by the oracle \emph{silently}, so per-arm operating points,
per-round dynamics, leak composition, and terminal outcomes are
all measured directly.

\emph{Instrumentation note.}  Completion budgets matter twice
over.  \emph{Generator side}: a pilot run at a 1{,}024-token
completion cap truncated $36$--$68\%$ of round-2 completions by
arm on HumanEval ($30$--$45\%$ on MBPP; first attempts: $4$--$6\%$
and $0$--$2\%$) into unparsable code, which masqueraded as severe
repair degradation; the reported runs use an 8{,}192-token cap,
under which unparsable rates fall to $0.8\%$ (HumanEval) and
$0.2\%$ (MBPP), with a per-attempt parse-health flag recorded.
\emph{Judge side}: the non-reasoning Qwen judges emit short
verdicts, and their 512-token verdict budget was never binding
(zero verdict-format failures across all same-family cells); the
thinking control's 4{,}096-token budget likewise never bound (two
MBPP tasks were lost to repeated API read timeouts and are

\subsection{Oracle verification never loses correct work}
Unit-test verification behaves exactly as the theory demands.
Leak is $.000$ in all four oracle arm-benchmark cells
(Theorem~\ref{thm:loop} at $b{=}1$), no correct solution is ever
re-rolled away, and the terminal-vs-own-first-attempt contrast is
uniformly positive: $+7$/$+5$ rescues on HumanEval ($p = .016$/
$.063$; the wrong-first pools are only 7--8 tasks) and
$+20$/$+17$ on MBPP (both $p < 10^{-4}$).  MBPP also displays the
budget side of the trade: with repair genuinely hard there
($\hat q \approx .17$), the oracle arms convert extra budget into
unresolved mass ($18$--$19\%$) rather than wrong deliveries.  At
$a = 1$ the backfire condition is vacuous; at $b = 1$ the
single-shot stream is exactly pure.

the causal comparison pairs each arm's terminal outcome against
\emph{its own} first attempt on the same task
(Table~\ref{tab:within}).

Table~\ref{tab:within} is the paper's cleanest contrast, and two
regularities organize it.  First, \emph{every same-family judge
cell is negative, and the four significant negative cells are all
same-family}: the isolated self-judge loses net $-15$ on HumanEval
($p = .0007$) and $-10$ on MBPP ($p = .021$); the
cross-capability judge---still Qwen-family---loses $-10$ on both
benchmarks ($p = .006$ each).  Second, \emph{no cross-family cell
is ever significantly negative}: deepseek-v4-pro trends positive on
both ($+3$/$+5$, n.s.), and glm-5.2 is \emph{positive} on MBPP
($+8$, nominal $p = .039$; $p_{\mathrm{del}} = .012$).  On
HumanEval the cross-family judges are near-oracles; on MBPP they
are lenient but harmless (Tables~\ref{tab:main},
\ref{tab:mbpp-main}).  The
harmful configuration is exclusively the middle one: a judge harsh
enough to reject correct work ($\hat a = .58$--$.77$) and, as
§\ref{sec:collapse} shows, persistent about it.

The same-family losses are not delivered errors but \emph{churned}
correct work: of the self-judge's 17 HumanEval losses, 16 end
unresolved and only one is delivered wrong; MBPP shows the same
shape (13 losses: 4
delivered-wrong, 9 unresolved).  The conversion channel that the
theory prices, $(1-a)p\pi_q$, has collapsed (HumanEval conversions
across the six judges: 3/1/0/0/0/1), because retry quality is no
longer binding: repairs succeed at $\hat q = .47$--$.95$.  What
fails is not the repair but the verifier's willingness to accept
it.  The churn shape also explains the $p_{\mathrm{del}}$ column:
excluding unresolved tasks removes exactly the tasks the verifier
destroyed, so a delivered-only convention cannot witness
churn-type losses by construction.

The judge configurations co-vary---the cross-family judges are
also stronger, \emph{reasoning} models---and the
\textsf{judge\_self\_iso\_thinking} control dissects the confound:
it serves the same qwen-plus family with thinking enabled and the
cross-family 4{,}096-token verdict budget.  On the family side it
behaves like its siblings---its acceptance still collapses and
clusters (§\ref{sec:collapse})---but on the outcome side it does
not: net $-3$ on HumanEval ($p = .58$) and $-4$ on MBPP
($p = .45$), against $-15$/$-10$ for its non-reasoning sibling.
The significant backfires therefore require the same-family
correlation \emph{and} the non-reasoning operating point, and the
difference lives in resubmission behavior: on MBPP the control
accepts $62\%$ of correct round-2 attempts against the plain
self-judge's $42\%$, so the churn never becomes a significant
loss.  The remaining budget asymmetry is
inert and biased against the winners: the non-reasoning Qwen
judges' 512-cap was never binding, while the cross-family
reasoning judges occasionally exhausted their own budgets
mid-thought, and the harness recorded those events as rejections
concentrated on correct attempts (excluding them, glm's
acceptance of correct work is $.96$ on HumanEval and $.94$ on
MBPP, against $.80$/$.88$ as measured inside the loop); verdict
costs differ widely (medians: $240$--$860$ tokens non-reasoning
Qwen, $860$--$1{,}480$ cross-family, $\approx 3{,}500$ control),
but the only asymmetry that exists points against the
cross-family arms.  We report nominal $p$-values throughout: under
Benjamini--Hochberg control across the twelve judge cells, the
HumanEval self-judge and both cross-capability backfires remain
significant, while the MBPP self-judge ($p = .021$) and glm's
positive effect ($p = .039$) do not---we read both as strongly
suggestive.  The pattern itself is complete sign separation: the
eight same-family cells are exactly the eight most-negative
effects of the twelve (exchangeability $p = 1/\binom{12}{8}
\approx .002$).

\subsection{The mechanism: per-round acceptance collapse}
\label{sec:collapse}
The same-family judges' pooled operating points look merely
mediocre ($\hat a = .58$--$.77$).  The per-round instrumentation
shows what the pool hides (Table~\ref{tab:ak}): acceptance of
\emph{correct} attempts collapses as rounds accumulate.  The
isolated self-judge accepts $82\%$ of correct first attempts
on HumanEval but $24\%$ of correct second attempts and
$0$--$22\%$ thereafter (MBPP: same shape; Table~\ref{tab:ak}).
The cross-capability
and context judges collapse similarly; late-round cells are
individually noisy ($n = 5$--$29$) but the trajectory is monotone
and replicates.
The thinking control collapses in family fashion ($.87 \to .45
\to .20$ on HumanEval, $.75 \to .62 \to .35$ on MBPP), though
less steeply than its non-reasoning siblings.  No cross-family
judge collapses: on parsed verdicts (\emph{Instrumentation
note}), deepseek-v4-pro on MBPP accepts correct work at $.84$
(round 1) and $.88$ (round 2), and glm-5.2's apparent HumanEval
decline vanishes---its round-2 acceptance among parsed verdicts
is $12/12$.

The isolated judges cannot see the round---their input is the
problem and the code, nothing else---so the collapse cannot be a
response to round information.  It is also not a harness artifact
in the arms where it appears: the same-family judges never
exhausted their verdict budgets (zero format failures), so every
same-family rejection in Table~\ref{tab:ak} is a parsed verdict.
Surviving-pool composition differs across arms, but the generator
and retry prompts are shared, and the cross-family judges accept
the same retry stream at a flat rate---neither code style nor
instrument explains a same-family-specific pattern.  A round-blind
judge that accepts fresh correct work at $.8+$ and survivors near
zero is responding to \emph{which tasks} survive: each rejection
round enriches the surviving pool for instance-persistent
low-$a_m$ modes, exactly the correlated-failure mixture of
Theorem~\ref{thm:decorr}.  This
is how same-family backfire persists at high repair quality:
repairs succeed ($\hat q = .47$--$.95$), but correct repairs on
hostile-mode tasks are re-rejected until the budget dies.

The rejections also cluster by task, as the mixture predicts.  On
HumanEval the isolated self-judge rejected \emph{every} correct
attempt on $19$ of the $32$ tasks where it saw more than one; its
per-task rejection counts are
overdispersed relative to a pooled binomial
($\chi^2/\mathrm{df} = 1.31$); the thinking control clusters
like its siblings ($\chi^2/\mathrm{df} = 1.03$, with $8$ of $24$
multi-correct tasks all-rejected).  The cross-family arms show no
such clustering on either benchmark
($\chi^2/\mathrm{df} = .60$--$.72$; deepseek-v4-pro on MBPP
rejected all correct attempts on $0$ of the $26$ tasks where it
saw more than one).  Selection makes this diagnostic imperfect---a
task must be rejected to keep producing rounds---so we read it as
evidence \emph{consistent with} instance persistence rather than
an identification of it; the contrast---same-family clustered,
cross-family diffuse, on both benchmarks---is what the theory
anticipates.

The terminal-backfire condition
(Corollary~\ref{cor:backfire}) prices the pooled parameters, and
Table~\ref{tab:backfire} shows both of its forms against the
data.  The exact finite-budget form, $A_5 < \hat p_1$, signs all
twelve cells: it flags the four significant backfires and clears
the eight cells that are not, the thinking control included
(under a pure sign labeling it additionally misses the four
negative-but-nonsignificant context and control cells).  The cross-capability arms are flagged clearly
($-2.5$/$-1.4$pp predicted against $-6.1$/$-4.0$pp observed),
because their retry quality is genuinely degraded on the hard
pool ($\hat q = .71$/$.51$ against $\hat p_1 = .95$/$.74$, below
$q^\ast$ under either rejection-pool estimate)---the degradation
regime of Theorem~\ref{thm:flip}.  The self-judge arms are
flagged only marginally ($-0.1$pp each, against $-9.1$/$-4.0$pp
observed), and the residual is the point: pooling averages away
the acceptance collapse of Table~\ref{tab:ak}, which is exactly
the difference between the model's $-0.1$pp and the measured
losses.  The steady-state reduction
$\hat R_q < (1-\hat a)/\hat b$ is the interpretable limit and is
sufficient-only---it agrees everywhere except the two self-judge
cells, which the finite-budget truncation term catches.  The two
theorems are complementary: the flip condition
(Theorem~\ref{thm:flip}) prices degradation-driven backfire, the
mixture (Theorem~\ref{thm:decorr}) prices correlation-driven
backfire, and the experiment measures both.

\subsection{Single-shot purification and the budget}
\label{sec:budget}
Truncating every run at budget $N$ and recomputing
conditional-on-delivery error traces the purity curve
(Table~\ref{tab:budget} in the appendix).  At $N{=}1$, all twelve
judge-benchmark
cells are purer than their baselines---exactly the chance-line
prediction of Theorem~\ref{thm:decorr}, since every pooled
operating point satisfies $\hat a + \hat b > 1$.  What the added
budget buys depends on the configuration.  For the cross-family
judges the stream barely moves; for the non-reasoning same-family
judges purity decays with budget as churned mass accumulates
(the control decays only mildly, $.007 \to .019$ HumanEval and
$.230 \to .247$ MBPP, below baseline throughout), and the
cross-capability arm on MBPP---lowest catch ($\hat b = .41$) and
harshest churn---is the only cell whose delivered stream crosses
the no-verification baseline (at $N{=}3$: $.267$ vs.\ $.259$).  On
HumanEval no arm crosses at any budget, though the self-judge
approaches it by $N{=}5$.  Budget
is thus not neutral (Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance} assumes
generator-rate retries).

\subsection{Pooled rates: a mild, family-associated gap}
\label{sec:pooled}
Pooled operating points understate observed terminal leak only
mildly in the clean runs.  Per-arm ratios
$\pi_{\mathrm{obs}}/\pi_{\mathrm{pred}}$ are $1.19$--$2.12$ on
HumanEval (geometric mean $1.72$, bootstrap 95\% CI
$[0.90, 3.33]$; leak counts are only $2$--$7$ per arm, and $22\%$
of resamples contain a zero-leak arm) and $1.05$--$1.39$ on MBPP
(geometric mean $1.19$, $[0.95, 1.43]$; both same-family arms
individually exclude 1).  A degradation-only model at
the measured per-round success trajectory reproduces the
same-family gaps in full on MBPP ($106$--$109\%$ of the
pooled-to-observed gap), so no miss-clustering channel is
required there; the cross-family arms' tiny gaps are not
separately explained ($72$--$75\%$) but sit within noise of zero.

Two lessons.  First, the \emph{magnitude} of pooled-rate failure
is instrumentation-sensitive: the same three HumanEval judge arms
that measure $2.1$/$1.8$/$1.7$ here measured $3.4$/$3.3$/$3.6$
under the pilot's 1{,}024-token cap.  Second, pooled $\hat a$
hides the acceptance collapse of §\ref{sec:collapse} in the
opposite direction: the pool says the self-judge accepts $58\%$
of correct work; the round-2 truth is $24\%$ and falling.  Pooled
rates are blind to the two per-round structures that drive the
loop.

\subsection{Feedback content: a paired null ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}
The within-run comparison of traceback vs.\ verdict-only feedback
is confounded by independent first draws, so we re-ran the
ablation \emph{paired}: for every task whose oracle-arm first
attempt failed, the same failing code was re-submitted to
two repair sub-loops differing only in feedback content
(verdict-only vs.\ full traceback; same generator, temperature,
and budget; McNemar exact on repair success).  The result is a
clean null: $5/8$ vs.\ $7/8$ repaired on HumanEval ($p = .50$),
$18/64$ vs.\ $21/64$ on MBPP ($p = .55$), pooled discordants $4$
vs.\ $9$ ($p = .27$)---the direction consistently favors
traceback but is far from significance, and the within-run
(unpaired) comparison is likewise null.  We conclude that
$\delta > 0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:delta}) remains open here:
feedback content is not detectably the lever at this sample size.

\subsection{No context-induced collapse}
The collapse motivation of §\ref{sec:mixture} predicts that a
judge shown the generator's own reasoning should be \emph{worse}.
On both benchmarks the context-sharing judge sits between the
isolated self-judge and the cross-family arms: a slightly better
operating point, per-round acceptance still collapsing
(Table~\ref{tab:ak}), and a non-significant within-arm deficit
($-6$, $p = .15$; $-4$, $p = .45$).  We find no evidence of context-induced collapse and cannot
exclude moderate harm; what is new here is that the
judge-configuration contrast dwarfs the context contrast:
correlation travels with the judge configuration, not the
prompt---it must be measured (Theorem~\ref{thm:decorr}), not
assumed.


\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Design guidelines.}
\textbf{G1: oracle-first.}  Wherever a deterministic check exists
(tests, compilers, schemas), attach it to the verifier: leak is
exactly zero in every oracle cell we measured, at bounded cost.
\textbf{G2: mind the configuration, not just the operating
point.}  The cheapest lever we measured is judge selection: no
cross-family configuration showed a significant backfire, and on
HumanEval the cross-family judges approached oracle behavior,
while same-family judges with similar operating points destroyed
correct work.  The thinking control shows both axes act---the
collapse signature travels with the family, significant damage
requires the weak non-reasoning configuration---so prefer a
strong verifier from another family; as a heuristic that held in
every cell we measured.  \textbf{G3: probe acceptance
persistence.}  Take correct solutions the judge has rejected and
resubmit them: if acceptance keeps falling by round
(Table~\ref{tab:ak}'s pattern), the correlated-failure mode is
active---change the verifier, not the prompt.  \textbf{G4: check the flip condition
where repair is genuinely hard.}  When retries succeed well below
the generator's rate (our cross-capability arm; oracle repair on
MBPP at $\hat q \approx .17$), Corollary~\ref{cor:calibrate}'s
catch-first advice inverts---protect acceptance
(Corollary~\ref{cor:backfire}).  \textbf{G5: never trust pooled operating points.}
They average away both per-round structures---retry degradation
and acceptance collapse (§\ref{sec:pooled}); report per-round
health.  \textbf{G6: budget is a verifier property.}
Single-shot purification held in all twelve judge cells
(§\ref{sec:budget}); every same-family degradation and the only
baseline crossing arrived with budget, a correlated verifier
converting extra budget into extra rounds of re-rejecting the
same correct work.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
One generator (qwen-plus) at one temperature; judges are one model
per family from three families, via rolling aliases (API
identifiers recorded);
cross-family arms are isolated-context only, so the family and
context levers are not fully crossed.  The family/capability
confound is dissected by the thinking control but not dissolved
to single ingredients: with one model per family and one
reasoning toggle, whether it is reasoning per se, verdict budget,
or another capability ingredient that mutes the damage remains
open---though the control's collapse shows the correlation
channel itself is family-associated.  Two code benchmarks with unit-test oracles:
correctness is measured relative to the benchmark test suites,
not exhaustively, so ``leak'' means suite-passing wrong work.
Same-family losses concentrate in unresolved tasks, so
delivered-error composition rests on small conversion counts
($\le 5$ per arm on HumanEval); late-round acceptance cells rest
on $n = 5$--$29$ attempts and are individually noisy, though the
monotone collapse replicates.  The acceptance-persistence statistics are
survivor-selected---consistent with, not identifying,
instance-persistent modes.  The paired feedback ablation
(§\ref{sec:ablation}; 72 pairs) is underpowered for small
$\delta$; $p$-values are nominal (FDR control: §\ref{sec:exp});
and $q$ is estimated from mixed rejection pools (the
correctly-rejected-pool estimate exceeds $q^\ast$ for every arm
but the cross-capability one).

\section{Conclusion}

Verification is not free reliability, and its failure modes have
structure: a verifier can hurt through degraded retries (priced by
the flip threshold $q^\ast$) or through correlation with the
generator (exposed by per-round acceptance), and pooled operating
points hide both.  In our study the second mode dominated: non-reasoning same-family
judges churned correct work to death while cross-family judges
sat near the oracle---and the thinking control shows the
signature alone does not suffice.  The practical summary:
\emph{before trusting a verifier, measure what it does to correct
work it has already rejected once.}

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Both experiments use qwen-plus as generator (temperature 1.0,
completion cap 8{,}192 tokens) with judges qwen-max and qwen-plus
(512-token verdict budget), deepseek-v4-pro and glm-5.2 (4{,}096;
reasoning models), and a qwen-plus reasoning control
(enable\_thinking, 4{,}096), served via a DashScope-compatible API
(October 2026); budget $N{=}5$; bootstrap seed 20261016.  Complete
per-attempt logs (1{,}476 HumanEval runs, 2{,}221 MBPP runs,
10.7M tokens; every attempt silently oracle-scored with parse
health), 144 paired-ablation variants, prompts, code, and
analysis scripts are in the supplementary material.
